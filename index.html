Debate formats we practise

British Parliamentary

Four teams of two in one room. Each team argues for its own position and against the other three.

Asian Parliamentary

Two teams of three with reply speeches. The format rewards clear structure and direct clash.

Oxford

Two teams argue for and against a motion. The audience votes before and after the debate, and the side that moves more minds wins.

Extempore and elocution

Short prepared and unprepared speeches in English, Bangla and Hindi for members who want to start smaller.

How a debate works

The motion

Every debate starts with a statement, such as This house would ban private tuition for students below Class 8. One side supports the motion and the other opposes it, whatever the speakers personally think.

Burden of proof

The proposition must show that the motion should be accepted. The opposition must show that it should not. Each side builds a case with definitions, arguments and examples.

Speeches

Speakers deliver timed speeches in a fixed order. The first speakers set out the case, the middle speakers extend it and answer the other side, and the last speakers summarise the debate.

Points of information

In parliamentary formats, a speaker on the other side may stand and offer a short question or challenge. The speaker holding the floor can accept or decline. The first and last minute of each speech are protected from points.

Rebuttal

Rebuttal means answering the strongest arguments of the other side, not the weakest. A rebuttal explains why an argument is wrong, why it matters less than claimed, or why it supports the other side.

Judging

In British Parliamentary debate, judges assess matter (the content of arguments), manner (delivery) and method (structure and use of time). Other formats weigh these elements differently.

Policy motions
Propose an action. Example: This house would make attendance optional in college.
Value motions
Ask whether something is good or bad. Example: This house believes that social media does more harm than good to student life.
Regret motions
Ask whether a past or present development was a mistake. Example: This house regrets the dominance of English in higher education.
Comparative motions
Ask which of two options is better. Example: This house prefers continuous assessment to final exams.

A short history of debating

Organised argument is older than most institutions that teach it. The main steps run as follows.

  1. 5th century BCE, Athens

    Both sides of every question

    Protagoras and other sophists taught citizens to argue. Protagoras held that every question has two opposing arguments, which is the idea behind assigning a debater a side. The assembly and the law courts made public speaking a civic skill.

  2. 4th century BCE, Athens

    Aristotle writes the Rhetoric

    Aristotle described three modes of persuasion: ethos (the credibility of the speaker), pathos (the emotion of the audience) and logos (the reasoning itself). Debate judges still use these ideas.

  3. Ancient India

    Shastrartha and the Nyaya tradition

    Public philosophical debate was a long-standing practice. The Brihadaranyaka Upanishad records Yajnavalkya answering challengers, among them Gargi. The Nyaya Sutra later separated debate into vada (a joint search for the truth), jalpa (debate aimed at winning) and vitanda (attack with no position of its own).

  4. 1st century BCE and 1st century CE, Rome

    Cicero and Quintilian

    Cicero practised and wrote about oratory. Quintilian wrote the Institutio Oratoria, a full programme for training a speaker from childhood.

  5. 12th and 13th centuries, Europe

    The medieval disputation

    Universities in Paris, Oxford and Bologna made the disputatio part of teaching. A master stated a position, answered objections and gave a ruling.

  6. 1815 and 1823, Britain

    The Cambridge Union and the Oxford Union

    Student societies adopted the manner of the House of Commons, with a motion, speakers for and against, and a vote. The Oxford Union also gave its name to the Oxford debate format.

  7. 1828, Calcutta

    Derozio and the Academic Association

    Henry Derozio, a teacher at Hindu College, led students in debates on religion, society and politics. The Academic Association was an early student debating forum in Bengal.

  8. 1981 onwards

    World Universities Debating Championship

    The first championship was held in Glasgow in 1981. It standardised British Parliamentary debating for university teams around the world.

  9. Today

    Colleges across Kolkata and Asia

    British Parliamentary is the format of the World Universities Debating Championship. Asian Parliamentary is widely used in tournaments across Asia. Oxford-style debates are common in public events and inter-college contests.
